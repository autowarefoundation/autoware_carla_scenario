    Metrics¶

    A run reads two kinds of quantity off the world, under keys the framework fixes:

    Reads Example An ODD maps onto it Watched by
    Measure (Scenario measures) A condition the ego drives in: what the scenario sets up, while it drives vehicle_ahead_gap_m Yes (measure: in a binding file) Coverage; a sweep draws a scenario's parameters from it
    Metric (this page) How the ego drove: an outcome, computed once the run is over collision_at_fault No MetricCondition

    An ODD describes the conditions a system is built to operate in, whatever it then does there. A metric reads what it did, so it has no place in one: were an ODD to map onto offroad, the ego's own driving would decide whether a run is inside the domain it is tested in, and an ODD excluding offroad would report a run that left the road as outside the ODD rather than as failed. scenario_measure("offroad"), measure: offroad in a binding file and register_measure("offroad", ...) are all refused.

    The built-in metrics¶

    They are the per-timestep driving-quality metrics of NVIDIA's AlpaSim (src/eval/src/eval/scorers), under AlpaSim's own names so results read the same in both, computed from CARLA's actors and map (autoware_carla_scenario.metrics, BUILT_IN_METRICS). An event reads 1 while it holds and 0 while it does not; the collisions, offroad and wrong_lane (Metric.boolean) are yes/no events, which the trajectory log writes as true and false (and the Rerun recording draws as states); every metric reads nothing without an ego, and those of the road nothing without a map. A metric reads one frame; AlpaSim folds a run's frames into one value with the aggregation in the last column (Metric.aggregation).

    Key Unit Reads AlpaSim folds with
    collision_any The ego's bounding box overlaps another vehicle's or a pedestrian's max
    collision_front ... and the other overlaps the ego's front edge max
    collision_rear ... its rear edge, and not its front max
    collision_lateral ... neither edge: a side swipe max
    collision_at_fault A front or a lateral collision max
    offroad A corner of the ego's box is on no driving lane max
    offroad_or_collision Off the road, or any collision max
    offroad_or_collision_at_fault Off the road, or a collision at fault max
    wrong_lane The ego heads more than 120 degrees away from the driving lane under its centre; nothing off the road max
    min_distance_to_obstacle_m m Box to box, to the nearest other vehicle or pedestrian; 0 touching, nothing when the ego is alone min
    min_distance_to_lane_boundary_m m From the ego's box to the edge of the driving lanes around it (lanes beside its own count while their near edge is within 6 m of its centre); 0 off the road min
    importance_sampling_weight ratio Not AlpaSim's: the run's importance-sampling weight so far, the product of NADE's p/q and (1-p)/(1-q) (AdversarialTrafficAction); 1 without NADE. Weight a batch of runs by its last value to estimate the naturalistic rate of what they count last
    lateral_reversals count Not AlpaSim's: how many times so far the ego's drift across its lane turned back by more than 0.1 m (Weaving) last
    lateral_wobble_m m Not AlpaSim's: how far the ego strayed across its lane about its course over the last 4 s, RMS (Weaving) max
    steering_reversals count Not AlpaSim's: how many times so far the steering applied to the ego turned back by more than 0.05 of full lock (Weaving) last
    turn_indicator_changes count Not AlpaSim's: how many times so far the ego's turn indicators changed, between off, left, right and hazard (Weaving) last

    Where AlpaSim's geometry is not CARLA's, the reading follows CARLA: AlpaSim asks whether the union of the lane polygons around the ego covers its box, CARLA whether a point is on a driving lane, so offroad asks it of the box's corners; the lane boundary takes the lanes beside the ego's as parallel strips as wide as CARLA says they are there, an approximation through a junction. Boxes are those of vehicles (vehicle.*) and pedestrians (walker.*); props are not road users. collision_* reads the boxes, not CARLA's collision sensor (CollisionCondition): it sees a collision as AlpaSim does, and tells the front from the rear.

    Weaving¶

    Three metrics read how the ego weaves from side to side, and a fourth how often its turn indicators changed. Unlike the others they read the run so far, not one frame: the runner hands the ego's motion to autoware_carla_scenario.metrics every tick (track_ego_motion), and they read what it has gathered.

    The ego's drift is how far it has moved across its lanes since the run began: its velocity across the lane under its centre, summed over time. The lane's heading is taken anew every frame, so following a bend is not a drift, and moving to the next lane is a drift of a lane's width with no jump at the lane line. Off the driving lanes the drift holds until the ego is back on one.

    • lateral_reversals counts the times the drift turned back by more than 0.1 m: it went right, then came back left by 0.1 m or more. This is the reversal count of driver-workload studies (Markkula and Engström, 2006). A lane change is one drift, however long, and adds nothing; a weave adds two a cycle. Under 0.1 m it is the noise of holding a lane.
    • lateral_wobble_m is how far the drift strayed about its course over the last 4 s: the RMS about the straight line that fits it best there. A weave of 0.25 Hz or faster turns back within the window and reads nearly in full (about 92% at 0.5 Hz); a lane change, slower, is mostly the course, and adds about a tenth of the lane's width while it lasts. It reads nothing before the third frame.
    • In a junction, whose lanes overlap, the drift holds, as it does off the driving lanes; a lane the other way is crossed the ego's way.
    • steering_reversals counts the times the steering applied to the ego (get_control().steer, 1 the vehicle's full lock) turned back by more than 0.05, about 3.5 degrees at the wheels of a car that locks at 70. It sees the controller's weaving before the vehicle shows it. Closed loop, a driver policy's controller moves its steer by a few hundredths while it holds a lane or a bend, which the gap leaves out; sawing through a turn is a tenth or more.

    turn_indicator_changes counts the changes of the indicators the ego shows (its CARLA light state: off, left, right, hazard), from the first frame on: a turn signalled and cancelled is two. It is counted apart from the steering, so an indicator that flickers through a turn and a steering that saws there each read on their own count. A vehicle's light state holds while an indicator is commanded, so its blinking is not a change.

    The counts are of the run so far, so a longer run reads more: compare runs of one scenario, or fail on a count, not on a rate:

    self.register_fail_condition(
    MetricCondition(LATERAL_REVERSALS, ComparisonRule.GREATER_THAN, 6, label="weaving")
)
self.register_fail_condition(
    MetricCondition(LATERAL_WOBBLE_M, ComparisonRule.GREATER_THAN, 0.5, label="weaving")
)

    CommonRoad metrics¶

    Fourteen more read how critical the frame is, with TU Munich's CommonRoad tools: CriMe's criticality measures and CommonRoad-Reach's reachable set (COMMONROAD_METRICS). Each is read on the ego against the road users around it, every one going on as it goes -- its velocity and acceleration held, a braking one stopping -- over a 3 s horizon; the CriMe measures against other road users are the most critical over them, as CriMe folds them.

    Key Unit Reads Folds with
    crime_ttc_s s Until the ego hits a road user ahead in its lane, both holding their acceleration (CriMe TTC); inf when they never meet min
    crime_thw_s s Until the ego reaches where the road user ahead is now (CriMe THW) min
    crime_hw_m m From the ego's front to the rear of the road user ahead, along the lane (CriMe HW) min
    crime_ttb_s s Left before the ego must brake at full to avoid a collision (CriMe TTB) min
    crime_ttce_s s Until the ego and a road user come closest (CriMe TTCE) min
    crime_dce_m m Between the ego and a road user when they come closest; 0 when they would collide (CriMe DCE) min
    crime_a_long_req_mps2 m/s^2 The longitudinal acceleration the ego needs not to hit the road user ahead, negative to brake (CriMe ALongReq) min
    crime_a_lat_req_mps2 m/s^2 The lateral acceleration the ego needs to steer clear of a road user (CriMe ALatReq) max
    crime_btn ratio The deceleration the ego needs not to hit the road user ahead over the most it can brake (11.5 m/s^2); above 1 braking alone no longer avoids it (CriMe BTN) max
    crime_stn ratio The lateral acceleration the ego needs to steer clear of a road user over the most it can steer (8 m/s^2); above 1 steering alone no longer avoids it (CriMe STN) max
    crime_ttr_s s How long the ego can go on as it goes before no evasive manoeuvre -- braking, kick-down, steering left or right, each simulated -- avoids the collision (CriMe TTR); at or under WTTR; inf when it hits no one, 0 when none does even now. Evaluated every 0.5 s by default min
    crime_wttr_s s How long the ego can go on as it goes before no way it can go is free of collisions to the end of the horizon, by CommonRoad-Reach's reachable set (CriMe WTTR); inf when it hits no one, 0 when no way is left even now min
    reach_drivable_area_m2 m^2 The road the ego can still reach without a collision at the end of the horizon (CommonRoad-Reach); 0 when every way it can go meets a road user: a collision it can no longer avoid min
    reach_drivable_area_sum_m2 m^2 The same, summed over the horizon's steps (CriMe's DA) min

    A measure with no one to measure from (no road user ahead for TTC) reads nothing, and so does every one while the ego is on no lanelet. A run failed on one is a safety_margin failure. They are watched like any other metric:

    from autoware_carla_scenario import ComparisonRule, MetricCondition
from autoware_carla_scenario.metrics import CRIME_TTC_S, REACH_DRIVABLE_AREA_M2

self.register_fail_condition(
    MetricCondition(CRIME_TTC_S, ComparisonRule.LESS_THAN, 1.5, label="TTC under 1.5 s")
)
self.register_fail_condition(
    MetricCondition(
        REACH_DRIVABLE_AREA_M2,
        ComparisonRule.LESS_THAN_OR_EQUAL,
        0.0,
        label="no way out",
    )
)

    CommonRoad's packages cannot share the framework's environment: commonroad-io pins protobuf 3.20, where the framework's gRPC code needs 4 or later, and CommonRoad-Reach builds for CPython 3.10 and 3.11 only. They run in worker processes of their own, the commonroad_metrics project at the root of the repository (commonroad_metrics/README.md), spoken to in JSON lines, on the frames a run recorded every 0.1 s of simulated time (How metrics are computed), the frames shared among several workers at once (four, or as many as the machine has cores). The workers read the world's map as CommonRoad: roadgen (>= 0.42.0, the map extra) converts the world's OpenDRIVE once per machine, cached under ~/.cache/autoware_carla_scenario/commonroad, and stay up for the next run on the map. A worker evaluates each frame moved to the ego and turned so the lane under it runs along +x: CriMe projects speeds onto a lane with the cosine of the lane's own heading, which is the speed along the lane only there. On a frame of ten road users the eight CriMe measures from TTC to ALatReq take about 95 ms together, BTN and STN 170 ms more, the drivable area about 155 ms (over 2 s instead of 3, 45 ms), and WTTR about 110 ms on a frame with a collision ahead, next to nothing on one without. TTR simulates the ego's manoeuvres: about 2 s a frame with a collision ahead.

    A slow metric is evaluated less often than every period_s (0.1 s): at its own period, metric_periods_s, by default 0.5 s for crime_ttr_s (DEFAULT_METRIC_PERIODS_S). A frame is asked only the metrics due on it, and a metric condition counts each NG sample until the next, so max_ng_seconds is still simulated time. Set the periods of any CommonRoad metric; the dictionary given replaces the one set, so start from it to keep the others':

    from autoware_carla_scenario.commonroad import (
    commonroad_settings,
    configure_commonroad_metrics,
)

configure_commonroad_metrics(
    metric_periods_s={**commonroad_settings().metric_periods_s, "crime_wttr_s": 0.3},
)

    A CommonRoad metric is computed only when a metric condition reads it, so a heavy one costs nothing until a scenario judges on it. The heavy ones (HEAVY_METRICS, crime_ttr_s today) can also be turned off for a run that should not wait on them, a quick iteration on a scenario that judges on TTR: a metric condition on one is then not judged -- the run neither fails nor passes on it, and a warning says so. They are on by default; turn them off with metrics.heavy=false, $AUTOWARE_CARLA_SCENARIO_HEAVY_METRICS=0, or configure_commonroad_metrics(heavy_metrics=False):

    uv run scenario scenario=intersection_passing/straight metrics.heavy=false
AUTOWARE_CARLA_SCENARIO_HEAVY_METRICS=0 uv run scenario scenario=intersection_passing/straight

    The scenario command (a single run, a batch, a sweep, and the SLURM tasks alike) decides in this order:

    1. metrics.heavy when a config sets it to true or false (the first case's of a batch that does): it overrides the environment variable;
    2. otherwise $AUTOWARE_CARLA_SCENARIO_HEAVY_METRICS: off when it is 0, false, off or no;
    3. otherwise on.

    The shipped config.yaml leaves metrics.heavy null, so the environment variable decides unless the command line or a scenario's config says otherwise. The command leaves your variable as it is and writes what it decided (1 or 0) to $AUTOWARE_CARLA_SCENARIO_HEAVY_METRICS_DECIDED (HEAVY_METRICS_DECIDED_ENV), which CommonRoadSettings reads before $AUTOWARE_CARLA_SCENARIO_HEAVY_METRICS: it carries the decision to the runs' own processes and to the SLURM jobs (launcher.env_passthrough's AUTOWARE_* passes it; a SLURM task runs with it rather than decide again from its own cases, which are only some of the batch's). Set by the command, not by you: the command never reads it to decide, and overwrites it each time it decides. A batch decides once, for all its cases; the cases of a Hydra --multirun (scenario=a,b) each decide for themselves, from the variable as you set it, not as an earlier case's metrics.heavy decided.

    The workers are started with, in order, the command in $AUTOWARE_CARLA_SCENARIO_COMMONROAD_WORKER, commonroad-metrics-worker on the PATH, or, in a checkout, uv run --project commonroad_metrics commonroad-metrics-worker (which builds its environment the first time). A run judged on a CommonRoad metric whose workers cannot be started fails with CommonRoadWorkerError rather than pass on a metric that read nothing. configure_commonroad_metrics() (in autoware_carla_scenario.commonroad) changes the period (period_s, and metric_periods_s for each metric), the number of workers, the horizon, the radius, CommonRoad-Reach's settings and the command.

    AlpaSim's other metrics need more than the world, and are not metrics here:

    AlpaSim metric Needs
    progress, progress_rel, dist_to_gt_trajectory, dist_to_gt_location, gt_dist_traveled_m The logged ground-truth trajectory of the ego
    min_ade@*, plan_deviation, open_loop_collision The driver's planned trajectories
    safety_monitor_triggered The driver's safety monitor
    img_is_black A camera image
    dist_traveled_m, duration_frac_20s, avg_dist_between_incidents The run, not a frame

    Judging a run on a metric¶

    MetricCondition(metric, rule, value, tolerance, *, max_ng_seconds, label) is a fail condition judged once the run is over. A frame is NG when the metric satisfies the comparison; the rule defaults to GREATER_THAN_OR_EQUAL and the value to 1, so an event metric on its own reads "it happens". The run fails when its NG frames add up to more than max_ng_seconds of simulated time -- with the default 0, at its first NG frame -- at the time they do. Each NG frame counts until the next frame evaluated (the last, the run's end, counts nothing); a frame the metric reads nothing in is not NG.

    from autoware_carla_scenario import ComparisonRule, MetricCondition
from autoware_carla_scenario.metrics import (
    COLLISION_AT_FAULT,
    CRIME_TTC_S,
    MIN_DISTANCE_TO_OBSTACLE_M,
    OFFROAD,
)


class MyScenario(BaseScenario):
    def setup(self) -> None:
        ...
        self.register_fail_condition(
            MetricCondition(COLLISION_AT_FAULT, label="collision at fault")
        )
        # Off the road for more than a second, in all.
        self.register_fail_condition(
            MetricCondition(OFFROAD, max_ng_seconds=1.0, label="off the road")
        )
        self.register_fail_condition(
            MetricCondition(
                MIN_DISTANCE_TO_OBSTACLE_M,
                ComparisonRule.LESS_THAN,
                0.5,
                label="closer than half a metre",
            )
        )
        self.register_fail_condition(
            MetricCondition(
                CRIME_TTC_S,
                ComparisonRule.LESS_THAN,
                1.5,
                max_ng_seconds=0.3,
                label="TTC under 1.5 s",
            )
        )

    Judged after the run, a metric condition neither ends a run early nor composes: PersistentCondition, NotCondition, AndCondition, OrCondition and StickyCondition refuse one (max_ng_seconds is how long it must hold), and so does register_pass_condition. A run that passed, or failed later than the metric did, fails on the metric: the earliest wins, its failure records the condition, when and what it found. Every metric condition's status in the result says what it found: ng_seconds, when it first went NG (first_ng_at) and its most critical value (worst). In the Scenario Editor it is the Metric card, under World, with its Max NG seconds.

    A run a metric condition fails is a failure of what the metric reads (Why a run failed): a collision metric collision, offroad and min_distance_to_lane_boundary_m lane_keeping, wrong_lane traffic_rule, min_distance_to_obstacle_m and the CommonRoad metrics safety_margin, and the weaving metrics and turn_indicator_changes stability. offroad_or_collision and its at-fault twin read two things and say neither, nor does the importance-sampling weight: register a condition on them with its category.

    How metrics are computed¶

    A run with a metric condition records its road users every tick -- each vehicle's and walker's place, velocity, acceleration and box, the ego's steering and light state, the importance-sampling weight (autoware_carla_scenario.metric_batch.FrameRecorder) -- and nothing more: the simulation never waits on a metric. When the run is over, while its CARLA map is still loaded, the frames are replayed: AlpaSim's metrics and the run's own every tick, in order, by the code that reads them off a live world, on a stand-in world answering with the recorded actors and the run's map; the CommonRoad metrics every 0.1 s, by the workers at once. Then each metric condition judges its metric's series. The metrics, and each metric condition's NG frames, go into the run's trajectory log (metric and condition records) and so into its Rerun recording (Metrics in the recording).

    To read an AlpaSim metric off a live world yourself -- in a probe, a test -- read_metric(key, world) returns its value in the frame, or None; a CommonRoad metric reads nothing there. evaluate_metrics(frames, carla_map, keys) computes any of them over recorded frames.

    Off the road as a fail condition¶

    offroad is a measurement, and a strict one: it reads 1 as soon as any one corner of the ego's box is on no driving lane. A corner over a gap between lanes at a junction entry, or a small offset between the OpenDRIVE lanes and where the vehicle drives, counts though the vehicle is in its lane, so MetricCondition(OFFROAD, ...) fails such runs (unless its max_ng_seconds lets them through). To judge a run by how much of the box is off instead, use OffRoadFractionCondition(fraction, entity_name, *, label), a condition rather than a metric:

    from autoware_carla_scenario import OffRoadFractionCondition

self.register_fail_condition(OffRoadFractionCondition(0.3, label="offroad"))

    Every tick it samples a 5 x 3 grid of points over the entity's bounding box (the ego's unless entity_name names another): 5 evenly spaced along its length and 3 across its width, the box's edges and corners included. A point is off when get_waypoint(point, project_to_road=False, lane_type=carla.LaneType.Driving) finds no lane there, asked at the height of the entity, so that where roads cross at different heights the lane is the one it is on. It fails once at least fraction (in (0, 1]) of the 15 points are off. The fraction is of points, not of area: one corner off is 1/15, the front edge 3/15, one long side 5/15. fraction=1/15 fails on any one point, the way the metric does on a corner; 0.3 fails at 5 points, so it tolerates a corner or the front edge over a lane gap and fails once a whole long side of the box is off. In the Scenario Editor it is the Off the road card, under Entity.

    A scenario or a Python ODD may import any name of autoware_carla_scenario.metrics -- the keys, Metric, BUILT_IN_METRICS, is_metric() and read_metric() -- and is checked against them like any other (Static Check). A Metric is frozen: assigning to one of its fields fails the check, as it fails the run. A scenario may build its own (Metric("my_metric", read)); the check calls its read with a world and refuses one that returns anything but a float or an Optional[float].
